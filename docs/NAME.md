# The name

A sapper is a combat engineer. The word comes from French *saper*, to undermine,
and *sappe*, a spade. In siege warfare sappers dug covered trenches, called saps,
toward a fortress under fire, and tunnelled beneath its walls to bring them down.
The British Royal Engineers adopted the title Royal Sappers and Miners in 1813,
and "sapper" is still the rank of a private soldier in several engineer corps.

Sappers knew better than anyone how walls fail, so they were also the ones who
built and repaired them.

sapper does the same to your backups. `sapper breach` restores the latest
snapshot into a throwaway directory and checks every file against the checksums
recorded at backup time. A backup that has never been restored is unbuilt: it
may exist, but nobody knows yet whether it would hold. sapper's job is to find
out before an attacker or a failed disk does.

The name describes a method. It does not claim any affiliation with a military,
government or intelligence organisation, and none exists.
